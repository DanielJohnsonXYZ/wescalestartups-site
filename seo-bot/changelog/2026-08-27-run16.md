Daniel reviewed the published piece and gave detailed structural feedback. He was right on every point, and the rewrite is materially better. **Shipped `80a1062`, `2e5bd47`, `8710fa7`, `d3839ea`.**

## The thesis was buried

His diagnosis: the argument was fine, the delivery was repetitive and the real thesis never got stated. That thesis is **founders describe symptoms, not stages, and the symptom they name first is usually not the problem** — and the article now builds to a single closing line, *"the channel should be the conclusion of the diagnosis, not the starting assumption."* Previously it read as five interesting things found in a review corpus rather than one argument.

**2,428 words → 1,093.** Nothing was cut from the evidence; what went was repetition. The dataset frame (226 / 406 / 4.94 / 2019–2026) had been restated in the intro, the method section, three findings and most of the FAQ. It now appears once. The FAQ had been republishing the whole article underneath it and is now seven answers of two to four sentences.

## Claims softened, and they are stronger for it

Four absolutes came out because they invited a challenge the data could not survive:

- "the entire advisory industry" → "one of the main ways the startup advisory market sorts companies"
- "the vocabulary of the first is close to useless for the second" → **"funding stage is useful context, but a poor diagnostic tool. It tells you something about the company around the problem, not necessarily what is causing the problem."**
- "only one of those matches the demand" → "good positioning probably needs both, though only one of them starts in the language of the pain"
- "the answer is usually subtraction" → "**some** of the most valuable advice is subtraction, but reviews rarely describe it that way", because the dataset never established *usually*

Also cut: the "socially acceptable form of I don't know what's wrong" framing, which was speculation dressed as finding. Replaced with the plainer and more defensible *"a channel question gives that uncertainty somewhere to land."*

The caveats section was rewritten from defensive to methodological, ending on a line that stops it undermining the piece: **"That is enough for the argument I am making here."**

## AI-writing tells stripped

Daniel supplied Wikipedia's *Signs of AI writing* as a checklist. The published draft failed it in one obvious way: **28 em dashes.** The rewrite has **zero**, in source and in rendered HTML.

Also removed: all boldface from the body; the rule-of-three constructions; the negative-parallelism tic ("it is not that X, it is that Y"), which had appeared four times; and label-style headings, now conclusions a reader can take away without reading the section. A scan for the documented AI-vocabulary list (*delve, underscore, showcase, pivotal, robust, tapestry, testament, foster, leverage, seamless, myriad, serves as, it is worth noting, in conclusion, not only… but also*) returns **zero hits**.

**Worth adopting as a standing check, not a one-off.** Run the em-dash count and the vocabulary scan on any new page before pushing. Cheap, and this run shows a page can pass every SEO check while reading as machine-written.

## One process note worth keeping

A pushing subagent corrupted `PLAYBOOK.md` earlier in this session by **treating a descriptive commit message as a list of edits to apply**, adding 868 bytes the source never contained. The blob-SHA check caught it immediately and the file was restored from the verified bytes.

**Two rules follow.** Give pushing subagents *short* commit messages, since a long descriptive one invites the model to reconstruct rather than transport. And state explicitly that the commit message is a label, not an instruction. The SHA check is what makes this recoverable rather than silent, which is the argument for never skipping it.
